# Reference: oil and gas entities, terms and traps, 2015–2026

Status of knowledge: early November 2026. This file covers the USA, Mexico, Venezuela, the Gulf, Iraq, Iran, the Levant, Egypt, Turkey, the EU-27 (plus Norway and the UK), Kazakhstan, Azerbaijan, Russian export routes, China and India. It lists value-chain events, benchmarks, institutions, sanctions, routes, companies, fields, refineries, terms in the main news languages, and false positives. Facts that change over time carry dates. "(unverified)" marks facts the research could not confirm. A range means sources disagree.

## 1. How to use this reference

**Labels (summary; the labelling instructions above take precedence over this file)**
- **core**: the headline reports or causes a change in production, flows, inventories, refinery runs or prices of crude, refined products, natural gas/LNG or petrochemical feedstocks. Typical cases: outages, OPEC+ decisions, oil/gas sanctions and price-cap changes, chokepoint closures, tanker attacks, inventory data, price moves, and retail fuel-price decisions.
- **related**: oil/gas industry context with no direct balance effect. Typical cases: company earnings, M&A, management changes, long-lead exploration or FIDs, petrochemical markets, legal or administrative commentary on sanctions, and macro-through-oil (inflation driven by fuel, petro-state budgets, currencies moving on oil revenue, stock indices moved by oil).
- **none**: lexical coincidences (cooking oil, tear gas, sports clubs, sponsorships, acronym clashes), and generic climate, renewables, power or coal news with no stated oil/gas link.

**Decision rules**
1. Require an **oil/gas anchor**. Any one of these counts:
   - a commodity word in its oil sense
   - a unit: barrels, b/d, bcm, mtpa, Mt
   - an entity from this file used in an oil context
   - a benchmark name together with a price
   - a strong verb: output, exports, loading, shut-in, force majeure, sanctions on oil
2. **Unambiguous entities** are anchors by themselves, so the label is at least related: Aramco, ADNOC, QatarEnergy, Pemex, PDVSA, Rosneft, Gazprom Neft, Transneft, Novatek, KazMunayGas, SOCAR, TPAO, BOTAŞ, ONGC, Sinopec, CNOOC, SOMO, NIOC, OPEC+. **Ambiguous names** need context (§9): Shell, Total, BP, Marathon, Chevron, MOL, IOC, CPC, BTC, API, EIA, Petrol, Delek.
3. **Retail fuel prices count as oil** even without the word "oil". Examples: gas prices, 成品油调价, motorine zam, لجنة التسعير, Spritpreise, ceny paliw, पेट्रोल-डीजल के दाम, tope de 24 pesos.
4. **LPG and cooking-gas cylinders count**: रसोई गैस, بوتاجاز, tüp gaz, gas LP, газовый баллон. Edible-oil news is **none**, even when it says "crude" (crude palm oil, कच्चा पाम तेल).
5. **2026 war context**: treat these as core even when the headline is framed as military news:
   - Hormuz, Kharg, Ras Laffan, Fujairah, Yanbu, the East–West pipeline, Bab el-Mandeb
   - tanker attacks or seizures, shipping blockades, escorts and convoys
   - energy force majeure
   - drone strikes on refineries, ports or CPC

   A military or diplomatic Iran or Russia headline with no energy, shipping or sanctions angle is related, not core.
6. **Check the date; entities change.** Examples:
   - UAE was in OPEC until 30 Apr 2026.
   - Pioneer was independent until May 2024.
   - Cepsa was renamed Moeve in Oct 2024.
   - Euronav merged into CMB.Tech in 2024–25.
   - US usage became "Gulf of America" in 2025.

   Do not judge plausibility by numbers: Gulf output and Hormuz flows in 2026 are far below the pre-war baselines given here.
7. **Normalize scripts**:
   - Arabic ي/ك vs Persian ی/ک; Persian ZWNJ
   - Kazakh ұ, қ, ә, ң, ө, і
   - Chinese simplified vs traditional (台灣中油)
   - Hebrew acronyms with gershayim (בז"ן)
8. **Sport, sponsorship, cooking and medical contexts override oil words.** These are all **none**: "النفط يفوز", Petrolul Ploiești, "Oilers beat Flames", Neftçi, MOL Fehérvár, "加油!".

**Units and abbreviations used here**
- mb/d = million barrels per day; kb/d = thousand barrels per day
- bcm = billion cubic metres; bcf/d = billion cubic feet per day
- Mtpa = million tonnes per annum (LNG); Mt = million tonnes
- FM = force majeure; FSRU = floating storage and regasification unit; SPM = single-point mooring
- STS = ship-to-ship transfer; SDN = OFAC blocked person; GL = OFAC general licence

## 2. Value chain taxonomy and which events move the balance

The balance identity is supply − demand = stock change. Headlines move it through four channels:
- **Supply**: OPEC+ decisions, outages, war, sanctions, shale activity.
- **Demand**: refinery runs, macro, Chinese import quotas, price-induced demand destruction.
- **Stocks**: SPR/IEA releases and refills, commercial and floating storage.
- **Flows**: chokepoints, pipelines, rerouting, exports.

| Segment | Headline events | Balance effect | Default label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upstream: exploration | licensing round, block award, seismic, discovery, appraisal well | long-lead | related (core only for a large near-term field or a policy shift) |
| Upstream: development | FID, project sanction, EPC contract, FPSO hire | adds supply in 2–6 years | related |
| Upstream: activity | rig count, frac spreads, DUCs, spudding, completions | leading indicator of US shale output | core-leaning |
| Upstream: production | output, start-up, first oil/gas, shut-in, FM, field blockade, strike, decline | direct | core |
| Midstream: pipelines | leak, outage, drone strike, restart, tariff/transit dispute, capacity expansion | upstream glut, downstream tightness | core (expansion plans: related) |
| Midstream: storage | Cushing draws/builds, tank farms, floating storage, SPR | inventory | core |
| Midstream: shipping | tanker attack/seizure/detention, rerouting, sanctions on vessels, freight spike | flows | core (freight rates alone: related) |
| Midstream: terminals | loading programmes, port closure, SPM damage, export halt | flows | core |
| Downstream: refining | runs/utilization, fire/outage (CDU, FCC, hydrocracker, coker), turnaround, closure, restart | crude demand ↓, product stocks ↓ | core |
| Downstream: margins | crack spreads, 3-2-1, refining margins | price signal | core (product price report) |
| Downstream: trade | product export quotas/bans (China, Russia), import tariffs | flows | core |
| Downstream: retail | pump-price decisions, fuel tax/excise cut, subsidy, price cap, rationing, queues/shortages | demand/price | core; policy debate only = related |
| Petrochemicals | steam crackers, ethylene, propylene, PDH, BTX, PX/PTA, PE/PP/PVC/PET, methanol, MTO, polymer prices | feedstock demand | related; core if crude, naphtha, ethane or LPG volumes are stated, or for integrated refinery runs (Chinese mega-complexes, teapots) |
| Gas: production and pipelines | field output, pipeline flows, transit contracts, cuts | gas balance | core |
| Gas: LNG | liquefaction train start-up/outage/FM, regas/FSRU arrival, cargo diversion, SPA signing | gas balance | core (long-term SPA: related, core-leaning) |
| Gas: storage | EU fill %, US EIA storage report | inventory | core |
| Policy and sanctions | designation, GL expiry, price-cap level, embargo, ban | flows | core if flow-affecting; legal commentary = related |
| Macro-through-oil | fuel-driven inflation, central bank citing oil, ruble/naira on oil revenue, oil-funded budgets, sovereign funds (SOFAZ, NBIM), equities moved by oil | indirect | related |

**Vocabulary (EN)**
- Products: gasoline (RBOB, CBOB, E10), diesel/ULSD, gasoil, heating oil, jet/kerosene, fuel oil (HSFO, VLSFO), bunkers, naphtha, LPG (propane, butane), NGLs, condensate, asphalt/bitumen, petcoke, base oils (borderline).
- Upstream: spud, tie-back, FPSO, FSO, EOR, API gravity, sweet/sour, light/heavy, associated gas, flaring, decline, PSA, farm-in/out.

**Usually none unless an explicit oil/gas link is stated**
- Coal, except coal-to-gas switching.
- Power, grids, nuclear, hydro. Exception: power cuts caused by a fuel/gas shortage (Egypt, Lebanon, Syria, Iraq) are related.
- Renewables, batteries, hydrogen.
- EVs: related only with a quantified oil-demand effect.
- Greenhouse gases and ETS, except field methane, flaring and ETS2 on road fuels.
- Climate pledges.

**Edge cases**
- Biofuels and renewable diesel (Neste MY, Diamond Green Diesel, Enilive): related.
- Heating oil: core-oil.
- City-gas explosions: related at most.
- Gas-station crime: none.
- Fuel-price protests (gilets jaunes 2018, Iran Nov 2019, Zhanaozen Jan 2022): related, or core if a price decision is reversed.

## 3. Global benchmarks, institutions and data releases

### 3.1 Crude benchmarks and grades
- **Brent** (برنت, Брент, 布伦特): Dated Brent (Platts; basket BFOE + Troll + WTI Midland since Jun 2023); ICE Brent futures (cash-settled). Dated Brent record about $144/bbl (Mar–early Apr 2026; one source $144.42). Futures path 2026: about $80 (2 Mar) → about $118 (late Mar) → about $70 (1 Jul) → >$100 (late Jul) → about $109 (early Sep) → $93–99 (early Nov; $94.60 on 4 Nov, $98.29 on 5 Nov).
- **WTI** (غرب تكساس, وست تگزاس, 美油): NYMEX CL, physically delivered at Cushing; WTI Midland, WTI Houston (MEH), Argus AGS. US grades: Mars, LLS, HLS, Southern Green Canyon, Poseidon, Thunder Horse, ANS, Bakken; ASCI (US sour index used for Saudi OSPs).
- **WCS** (Western Canadian Select, Hardisty/Houston; discount narrowed after TMX, May 2024).
- **Gulf sour**: Dubai/Oman (Platts Dubai partials; Oman futures on GME, formerly DME, rename unverified); **Murban** (ICE Futures Abu Dhabi since 2021). Saudi OSPs monthly about the 5th.
- **Russian**: **Urals** (Юралс; price-cap reference; average $90.7 in Sep 2026, about $23 under Brent), **ESPO** (ВСТО, Kozmino, mainly to China), Siberian Light, Sokol (Sakhalin-1), ARCO, Novy Port, Varandey.
- **Caspian**: **CPC Blend** (КТК Блэнд / смесь КТК; Kazakh, not sanctioned, exposed to drone strikes), **KEBCO** (Kazakh grade kept separate from Urals since 2023), **Azeri Light / Azeri BTC**.
- **Americas**: Maya, Istmo, Olmeca, MME (Mexico); Merey 16 (Venezuela); Tupi/Búzios (Brazil).
- **Others**: Johan Sverdrup, Es Sider, Saharan Blend, Bonny Light, Forcados; **INE SC** (上海原油期货, yuan-denominated futures since Mar 2018).
- Country grades (Arab Light/Medium/Heavy, Basrah Medium/Heavy, Kirkuk/KBT, KEC, Qatar Marine/Land, Iranian Light/Heavy, Mexican and Venezuelan grades) are in the country sections.

### 3.3 Spreads and structure
- Brent–WTI: transatlantic arbitrage. A wide spread means more US exports and lower US crude stocks.
- Backwardation (tight market) vs contango (glut; encourages storage and floating storage).
- Calendar spreads: CL1–CL2, "Dec-red".
- Margins and differentials: 3-2-1 crack, gasoline/diesel cracks, EFS (Brent–Dubai), Brent CFDs, DFL, Urals and ESPO discounts.

### 3.4 OPEC and OPEC+
- **OPEC** (أوبك, اوپک, ОПЕК, 欧佩克 / 石油输出国组织, OPEP, Opep).
  - Membership changes: Indonesia rejoined Jan 2016 and suspended again Nov 2016; Gabon rejoined 2016; Equatorial Guinea joined 2017; Congo joined 2018.
  - Exits: Qatar (1 Jan 2019), Ecuador (1 Jan 2020), Angola (1 Jan 2024), **UAE (announced 28 Apr 2026 via WAM, effective 1 May 2026, after 59 years)**.
  - **Members Nov 2026 (11, derived)**: Algeria, Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Libya, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, Venezuela. Iran, Libya and Venezuela are exempt from quotas.
- **OPEC+ / DoC** (Declaration of Cooperation, Dec 2016; أوبك+ / أوبك بلس, اوپک‌پلاس, ОПЕК+, 欧佩克+, OPEP+).
  - Non-OPEC members: Russia, Kazakhstan, Oman, Azerbaijan, Bahrain, Brunei, Malaysia, Mexico, Sudan, South Sudan.
  - Brazil joined the Charter of Cooperation in 2025 (no quotas).
  - UAE in OPEC+ until 30 Apr 2026.
- **Bodies**: OPEC Conference; ONOMM (ministerial); JMMC (Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee, every two months); monthly meeting of the voluntary-cut subgroup ("V8" until Apr 2026, "group of seven" from May 2026).
- **Cut layers**:
  - Apr–May 2020: historic 9.7 mb/d cut.
  - Oct 2022: group-wide 2 mb/d cut.
  - Apr 2023: voluntary 1.65 mb/d.
  - Nov 2023: voluntary 2.2 mb/d by eight countries; unwound Apr–Sep 2025.
  - 1.65 mb/d tranche: unwinding from Oct 2025 at about 137 kb/d per month, paused Q1 2026 (details unverified).
  - Compensation cuts (خطط التعويض) by overproducers Iraq, Kazakhstan and the UAE, 2024–25. The UAE won a +300 kb/d baseline phased in from 2025.
- **2026 decisions**:
  - April meeting: +206 kb/d for May.
  - After the UAE exit, the "seven" are Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman.
  - Aug 2026: +188 kb/d (low-quality source).
  - 4 Oct 2026: November required production held — KSA 10.5, Russia 9.9, Iraq 4.4, Kuwait 2.7, Kazakhstan 1.6, Algeria 1.0, Oman 0.8 mb/d.
  - 1 Nov 2026: December held at September levels. Next meeting 29 Nov 2026.
  - Actual Gulf output is far below targets because of Hormuz. The capacity review for 2027 baselines was delayed by the war.
- **Trap**: "eight OPEC+ countries" in a post-May-2026 headline is historical or an error. Kazakhstan says it will remain in OPEC+ but not join OPEC.
- **Related bodies**: OAPEC (أوابك, Kuwait); GECF (Gas Exporting Countries Forum, Doha, "gas OPEC", no quotas); IEF (International Energy Forum, Riyadh; runs JODI). The OPEC Fund for International Development (صندوق أوبك للتنمية الدولية) is development finance: none/related.

### 3.5 IEA, EIA and other data
- **IEA** (وكالة الطاقة الدولية, آژانس بین‌المللی انرژی, МЭА, 国际能源署, AIE; not the IAEA):
  - Monthly OMR (mid-month).
  - Collective stock releases: two in 2022, then **11 Mar 2026: 400 mb, the largest ever** (about 426 mb committed; about 330 mb released by Oct; peak about 2.5 mb/d in May).
  - Jun 2026 view: 2026 demand −1.1 mb/d.
- **EIA**:
  - **WPSR**: Wed 10:30 ET (Thu after a Monday holiday). Covers crude ex-SPR, Cushing, gasoline, distillates, utilization, imports/exports, product supplied.
  - **WNGSR**: Thu 10:30 ET.
  - **STEO**: monthly; absorbed the Drilling Productivity Report in 2024.
  - Also PSM, EIA-914, AEO.
- **Weekly market data**:
  - **API** Weekly Statistical Bulletin: Tue 4:30 pm ET.
  - **Baker Hughes rig count**: Fri 1 pm ET. Also Primary Vision frac spreads and the Dallas/Kansas City Fed energy surveys.
  - **CFTC COT**: Fri 3:30 pm ET (ICE publishes the Brent COT).
- **Other sources**:
  - Monthly reports: OPEC MOMR (mid-month, secondary sources); China GACC customs (about the 7th–10th) and NBS throughput (mid-month); India PPAC; Japan PAJ.
  - Weekly stocks: Fujairah FEDCom, ARA Insights Global, Singapore Enterprise.
  - EU data: AGSI+/ALSI, Weekly Oil Bulletin; JODI.
  - Tracking and incidents: Kpler, Vortexa, Kayrros, Windward, TankerTrackers, IMF PortWatch; UKMTO, JMIC.
  - Monthly OSPs: Aramco, SOMO, KPC, NIOC, QatarEnergy.
- **US SPR** (4 sites: Bryan Mound, Big Hill, West Hackberry, Bayou Choctaw; capacity 714 mb):
  - 2022: 180 mb sale; refills 2023–24.
  - 2026: the US share of the IEA action was 172 mb, done as **exchanges** (216 mb+ returned from early 2027). DOE said on 29 Oct 2026 that its awards complete the 172 mb.
  - Stock about 282.6 mb in the week to 23 Oct 2026, the lowest since 1982. A conflicting low of 284.6 mb is reported for 18 Sep.
  - 40 mb exchange RFP for Nov–Dec 2026. SPR headlines are core.
- **Other strategic stocks**:
  - China 国家石油储备: levels are secret; the 2025 Energy Law obliges company stocks.
  - India ISPRL: Visakhapatnam, Mangaluru, Padur (5.33 Mt).
  - Japan released 80 mb (2026).
  - EU 90-day stocks: EBV, SAGESS, CORES, APETRA, RARS, MSZKSZ, NORA, ENSE, ELG, Huoltovarmuuskeskus, SSHR.

## 4. Sanctions, price cap and shadow fleet

### 4.1 Russia: US measures
- **2022**: US ban on imports of Russian oil (Mar 2022). G7 price cap from 5 Dec 2022.
- **10 Jan 2025**: OFAC designated **Gazprom Neft** (Газпром нефть) and **Surgutneftegaz** (Сургутнефтегаз) as SDNs, plus about 180 tankers.
- **22 Oct 2025**: OFAC designated **Rosneft** (Роснефть) and **Lukoil** (ЛУКОЙЛ) plus 30+ subsidiaries under EO 14024, with a secondary-sanctions warning to foreign banks.
  - Wind-down licences ran to 21 Nov 2025.
  - GL 129: Rosneft Deutschland. GL 130: Lukoil's Bulgarian entities.
  - **GL 131 series** covers the sale of Lukoil International GmbH (LIG): 131E to 30 May, 131H to 22 Aug, then to 22 Oct. **GL 131K (21 Oct 2026) covers negotiations only.** Separate GLs for Lukoil retail stations abroad and the Bulgarian assets ran to 29 Oct 2026 (renewal unverified).
- **Lukoil sale saga**:
  - Nov 2025: Gunvor's bid failed after the US Treasury called it the "Kremlin's puppet".
  - Jan 2026: Lukoil agreed to sell most foreign assets to **Carlyle**. OFAC approval was needed, and the deal reportedly lapsed in summer 2026.
  - Nov 2026: a group led by **Todd Boehly** was reported close to a deal (unverified).
  - Lukoil impaired LIG by about 1.66 trn rubles.
  - Kazakh stakes (Karachaganak 13.5%, CPC 12.5%, Tengiz 5%) were excluded from the mandatory sale. Kazakhstan claimed a pre-emptive right (Feb 2026).
  - Exposed assets: Burgas (BG), Petrotel (RO), ISAB (IT; sold 2023), Zeeland 45% (NL), Teboil (FI), Lukoil Baltija, West Qurna-2 (Iraq), Litasco (trading arm).
- **The US price cap remained at $60.**

### 4.5 Shadow fleet
**Definition.** Ageing tankers with opaque ownership, flags of convenience or false flags, and no International Group P&I cover. They use AIS spoofing or "going dark", and dark STS transfers.

**Terms by language**
- EN shadow / dark / ghost fleet
- RU теневой флот; AR أسطول الظل / الأسطول المظلم; FA ناوگان سایه / ناوگان تاریک / شبح; ZH 影子船队 / 暗船队

### 4.7 Venezuela
- **Sanctions architecture**:
  - EO 13692 (2015); EO 13808 (Aug 2017, financial); EO 13850 (2018).
  - PDVSA designated an SDN on 28 Jan 2019; EO 13884 (Aug 2019, blocks the Government of Venezuela).
- **Chevron and oil licences**:
  - GL 41 (Chevron): 26 Nov 2022.
  - GL 44 (Oct 2023, broad relief after the Barbados Agreement): lapsed Apr 2024 (wind-down GL 44A).
  - GL 41 revoked in Mar 2025, wind-down to 27 May 2025. A restricted Chevron licence followed in Jul 2025 (no cash to Maduro; exports to the US fell to about 100 kb/d).
- **Secondary tariff**: EO 14245 (Mar 2025) threatened a 25% tariff on buyers of Venezuelan oil. It was IEEPA-based and probably void after the Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling (unverified).
- **Late 2025**: Cartel de los Soles designated an FTO; US naval build-up in the Caribbean; "blockade" of sanctioned tankers in Dec 2025; tanker seizures.
- **3 Jan 2026**: US forces captured **Nicolás Maduro**. **Delcy Rodríguez** became interim president on 5 Jan and cooperated with Washington from late January. The US took control of oil sales, with revenues held in US Treasury accounts under **EO 14373**.
- **2026 general licences**:
  - **GL 46** (29 Jan 2026, later 46E): "established US entities" may trade Venezuelan oil. Excludes upstream, debt swaps, gold/crypto payments and counterparties tied to Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba or China.
  - **GL 47** (3 Feb): US diluent exports.
  - **GL 49** (13 Feb): negotiating contingent upstream contracts.
  - **GL 50/50A** (13 Feb): operations of BP, Chevron, Eni, Repsol, Shell and Maurel & Prom.
  - **GL 52** (18 Mar 2026): the broadest PDVSA authorization. PDVSA bonds, debt and equity stay excluded, and the core sanctions remain.
- **CITGO**: in a court auction (Delaware, Crystallex case), Amber Energy (Elliott affiliate) won in late 2025. OFAC licensing of the closing is unverified.

### 4.8 Secondary tariffs and trade measures
- **India**: the US imposed an extra 25% tariff over Russian oil from 27 Aug 2025 (total 50%). It was removed from 7 Feb 2026 (EO of 6 Feb) under a trade deal (reciprocal tariff 18%). India committed to stop buying Russian oil (snapback possible), but compliance was partial.
- **China**: 10% tariff on US crude and 15% on US LNG and coal from 10 Feb 2025.
- **US tariffs struck down**: the US Supreme Court ruled 20 Feb 2026 in *Learning Resources v. Trump* (6–3) that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs. The IEEPA tariffs on Canada and Mexico ended, including the 10% tariff on Canadian energy. A 10% global tariff under Section 122 applied for 150 days from 24 Feb 2026. Section 232 steel tariffs (which hit OCTG and pipe) remain.
- **Syria**: Caesar Act sanctions repealed or eased in late 2025 (unverified).

## 5. Chokepoints and cross-border routes

### 5.1 Chokepoints
- **Strait of Hormuz**. Names: مضيق هرمز (AR), تنگه هرمز (FA), Hürmüz Boğazı (TR), מצרי הורמוז (HE), Ормузский пролив (RU), 霍尔木兹海峡 (ZH), होर्मुज जलडमरूमध्य (HI), Straße von Hormus (DE), détroit d'Ormuz (FR), stretto di Hormuz (IT), estrecho de Ormuz (ES), cieśnina Ormuz (PL).
  - Pre-war: about 20 mb/d, about 20% of world oil consumption and about one fifth of LNG trade; about 125 large ships a day.
  - **2026**: IRGC warnings from 28 Feb; closure declared 2–4 Mar; traffic fell to about 5 ships a day.
  - Flow figures conflict: about 2.7 mb/d Mar–May (IEA); about 7.6 mb/d in Aug; crude back to about 13.5 mb/d in late Sep under US escorts; about 14 mb/d (82% of pre-war) in the week to 18 Oct (Kpler via Wikipedia); 7.3 mb/d in the week to 29 Oct (Kpler via CNBC).
  - Cumulative losses exceeded 1.3 bn bbl. Tanker attacks and mines were reported into Nov 2026.
  - **Islamabad Memorandum** (17 Jun 2026): Iran demands that seven of its conditions be met before reopening.
  - US escort operation "Project Freedom": 4–6 May. JMIC widened the route near Oman on 27 Jun.
- **Bab el-Mandeb / Red Sea** (باب المندب, البحر الأحمر, Babülmendep, Kızıldeniz, הים האדום, Баб-эль-Мандеб).
  - About 8.6 mb/d in 2023, about 4 mb/d in 2024 after Houthi attacks from Nov 2023 (Galaxy Leader seizure; Operation Prosperity Guardian Dec 2023; EU Aspides; the Sounion fire 2024).
  - **2026**: from 20 Jul the Houthis declared a "maritime embargo" on Saudi Arabia. They struck the Jizan refinery and Yanbu (26 Jul), hit a tanker 63 nm west of Yanbu (Aug), and attacked the ship Tihamah (6 killed, Aug). They claimed control of Yemen's whole Red Sea coast including Perim (جزيرة ميون, 11 Sep).
  - Saudi Arabia launched a "Multinational Maritime Security Alliance" (Aug 2026).
- **Suez Canal / SUMED** (قناة السويس, هيئة قناة السويس; Süveyş Kanalı; תעלת סואץ; Суэцкий канал).
  - Suez + SUMED oil flows: 8.8 mb/d (2023) → 4.9 mb/d (H1 2025).
  - Suez Canal Authority revenue: $4.67 bn in FY2025/26 (+23%); Aug 2026 $567.1 mn, the best month since Dec 2023, on rerouted tankers. Chairman Osama Rabie (أسامة ربيع).
  - **SUMED**: Ain Sokhna → Sidi Kerir, operated by Arab Petroleum Pipelines Co. (الشركة العربية لأنابيب البترول).
- **Turkish Straits** (İstanbul Boğazı/Boğaziçi, Çanakkale Boğazı, Türk Boğazları; Босфор). About 3–3.4 mb/d, mostly Russian and CPC crude from Novorossiysk. The Montreux Convention applies.
- **Danish Straits / Gulf of Finland** (Storebælt, Øresund, Skagen; Soome laht): Russian Baltic exports from Primorsk and Ust-Luga; shadow-fleet inspections.
- **Others**: Malacca (largest Asian volume route); Panama Canal (2023 drought restrictions on LNG and products); Cape of Good Hope (رأس الرجاء الصالح, Ümit Burnu; the rerouting path); Gulf of Aden (خليج عدن).

### 5.2 Hormuz bypass routes
- **East–West Pipeline / Petroline** (خط أنابيب شرق-غرب / بترولاين), about 1,200 km Abqaiq → **Yanbu** (ينبع).
  - Design 5 mb/d; Aramco announced 7 mb/d in 2025 (never tested at sustained rates); Yanbu terminals limit loadings to about 4.5–5 mb/d.
  - 2026: Yanbu exports rose from about 2 to over 5 mb/d. The line was shut after drone attacks on 13 Sep, restarted about 22 Sep, and was pumping 5.8 mb/d in early Oct.
  - Plans for about 10 mb/d bypass capacity within 2–3 years.
- **ADCOP / Habshan–Fujairah** (خط حبشان-الفجيرة): about 1.5–1.8 mb/d. A second line is planned for 2027. Fujairah (الفجيرة) was struck about 19 times between Feb and May 2026 (tank fires on 3, 14 and 16 Mar; loadings suspended).
- **Goreh–Jask** (خط لوله گوره-جاسک): designed for 1 mb/d, little used before 2026.
- **IPSA** (Iraq Pipeline through Saudi Arabia): mothballed since 1990; revival discussed.
- **Basra–Aqaba** (خط البصرة-العقبة): proposed, about 1 mb/d; stalled for years.
- **Kirkuk–Ceyhan** and Iraqi trucking through Syria (2026): see Iraq.
- Total available bypass capacity is about 3.5–5.5 mb/d (IEA); only Saudi Arabia and the UAE have one.
